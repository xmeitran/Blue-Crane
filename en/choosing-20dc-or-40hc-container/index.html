20DC or 40HC: Which Container Suits Your Shipment?

14/11/2026

A comparison of 20DC and 40HC containers by dimensions, indicative payload and suitable cargo, to help you choose the right transport option.

The 20DC (20 ft dry container) and 40HC (40 ft high cube) are the two dry container types most widely used in domestic and cross-border transport. Choosing the right one helps optimize cost, reduce damage and avoid exceeding permitted weights. This article compares the two based on commonly quoted standard specifications.

Indicative Specifications

The figures below are approximate and for reference only. Actual dimensions and payload vary by carrier, manufacturer and individual container.

When to Choose a 20DC

  • Heavy, dense cargo such as steel, stone, compact machinery or heavy bagged goods, which usually reaches the weight limit before filling the space.
  • Shipments of moderate volume that would not fill a 40 ft container.
  • Pick-up or delivery sites with limited access where long container trucks struggle to turn.

When to Choose a 40HC

  • Light, bulky cargo such as furniture, plastics, packaging, garments and consumer goods, which needs more volume than weight capacity.
  • Tall cargo that can use the extra height of a high cube container.
  • Large shipments where one 40HC is more efficient than two 20DCs.

Other Factors to Consider

  • Road weight limits: on roads in Vietnam and Cambodia, the combined weight of vehicle and cargo must comply with vehicle and road limits, so the actual cargo weight may be lower than the container’s maximum payload.
  • Packaging: package dimensions, stackability and lashing affect how much cargo fits.
  • Door dimensions: the door opening is smaller than the internal space, so check large packages in advance.
  • Oversized cargo: if the cargo exceeds dry container dimensions, a flatbed trailer or another option may be considered.
  • Total cost: compare freight, lifting charges, procedure fees and stuffing costs rather than container freight alone.
